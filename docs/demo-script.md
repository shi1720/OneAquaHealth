# Rill — four-minute demonstration

Read the voiceover verbatim. Screen directions are separate and are not spoken. Target: about four minutes at a calm pace, with short pauses while interacting. The scenario is synthetic; use the application's isolated demo workspace and do not imply these events occurred in the real world.

## Recording preparation

- Use a clean browser at 1440 × 900 or similar. Hide bookmarks, notifications, and personal account details.
- Open a fresh demonstration workspace. Rehearse the whole sequence once before recording.
- Use the same non-emergency concern for the observation, review, response, and recheck; choose a litter observation for an easy, safe story. Serious signs are useful to show the planner's deferral behaviour separately.
- Set the planner to **120 minutes**. Show the returned used minutes rather than saying that every minute is allocated.
- Record a genuine recheck result only in the synthetic demo, explicitly as part of that fictional scenario. Never present a fabricated real field visit.
- Use the final UI's exact labels. Do not claim an action happened externally merely because a task was recorded.
- Show the app for most of the video. Finish with the repository and a readable end card: **Rill · Shivam Gupta · OneAquaHealth · Track 2**.

## Voiceover

Hello, I'm Shivam Gupta, the creator of Rill.

A resident notices something unusual in a stream and reports it. But a report is only the beginning. Someone still has to decide what needs checking, who can check it safely, and whether anything happened afterwards.

Rill answers one practical question: with two hours available, what should our team check next?

This is an isolated demonstration workspace. Its observations are synthetic. They illustrate the workflow; they are not real environmental findings.

I'll start with a citizen observation. I choose the stream, record when I visited, and describe what I saw. Here, it's litter beside the water. I can add a photograph, describe the flow and clarity, and say how certain I am.

The important detail is that I don't have to guess the cause. Rill preserves my uncertainty.

Now the coordinator sees the observation in context. Verification priority and evidence strength are separate. The explanation shows why this report deserves attention, what information is missing, and what the next check should answer.

It also connects the concern to habitat, animal contact, and how people use this place. Those are reasons to investigate. They are not a diagnosis or a declaration that the water is safe.

Here's the signature interaction: plan our next two hours.

Rill selects checks within the time budget and explains the selection. It also shows deferred work. Serious signs and unsafe conditions need coordinator or expert handling; they don't become ordinary volunteer visits just because they scored highly.

I can turn this plan into tracked tasks. Each has a purpose, an owner, and a due date.

Next, I review the case and record the decision. When a task is completed, the result is captured. I then schedule a recheck, because recording an action does not prove the problem has improved.

The activity history keeps those steps connected. A coordinator can explain what was reported, what was decided, and what still needs attention.

The information can leave Rill, too. We support standard data exports and an experimental FHIR R4 environmental mapping, with locations and observations rather than invented patient records. This is a starting point for interoperability, not a certification claim.

Underneath, Rill uses versioned deterministic rules. There is no trained AI model making environmental judgments. The reasoning is visible, the core needs no paid AI key, and the human reviewer remains in control.

Our first customer hypothesis is the coordinator of a river group or monitoring programme. Volunteers would contribute free. A proposed managed subscription would fund coordination, hosting, and support.

Before claiming commercial or ecological impact, we'd run a supervised pilot: measure coordinator time, time to assignment, and how many concerns receive a documented recheck. We are not claiming customers or field validation today.

Rill's contribution to OneAquaHealth is a practical bridge from citizen evidence to accountable follow-through.

Because a stream concern should not disappear after someone presses Submit.

## Short spoken pitch

I'm Shivam Gupta, the creator of Rill. Citizen science gives us more eyes on our streams, but a coordinator still has to decide what to check next. Rill turns uncertain observations into an explainable fieldwork plan for the time a team actually has, then tracks the person responsible, the response, and the recheck. It uses transparent rules, keeps humans in control, and exports environmental evidence for other systems. Our goal is simple: make every useful stream concern lead to a checked outcome.

## Claims to avoid while recording

Do not say “AI detects pollution,” “the water is safe,” “we prevented illness,” “we have a municipal partnership,” “we saved a measured percentage,” “FHIR certified,” or “first in the world.” Do not call task estimates optimal walking routes. The planner optimises its stated deterministic selection policy, not real-world ecological outcomes.
